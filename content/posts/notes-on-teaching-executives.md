---
title: "Notes on teaching executives"
date: 2026-05-28
summary: "Senior people don't need the concept explained. They need permission to admit which part they never understood, and a worked example from their own P&L."
---

Some observations from running sessions with senior operators, mostly things I
got wrong first.

**They already know the concept.** Every P&L owner has read the same articles.
Explaining what optimisation *is* wastes the first forty minutes and signals you
don't know your audience. Start at the second question: where it fails, and
why.

**The real barrier is admitting the gap.** Somewhere in the chain there's a step
a senior person has nodded along to for years without following. In a room of
peers, nobody asks. The single most useful thing you can do is make it
normal — take the unglamorous step and work it through slowly, as if it were
obviously the hard part. It usually is.

**Generic examples don't land.** A case study about a European retailer is
entertainment. The same logic applied to their own network, with their own cost
structure, is uncomfortable — and uncomfortable is the state in which people
actually change something. This means preparation is most of the work, and it
doesn't scale, which is a genuine constraint rather than a problem to solve.

**Nobody remembers the framework.** Two weeks later what survives is one
sentence and one number. Choose them deliberately, because they're the entire
deliverable.
