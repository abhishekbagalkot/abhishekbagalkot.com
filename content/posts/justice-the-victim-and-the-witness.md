I am distressed by the political & societal happenings in the SC/ST Act and the IIT Bombay case.

Our government is supposed to care for us citizens. But it feels like it’s ignoring us. Not acting or even acknowledging the unfairness even when there is much uproar. It feels like the government is happy to see a certain section of society suffer, if it’s a means to win elections.

Why am I personally distressed? One may ask. Am I the target of this SC/ST Act? Because I am a Brahmin man? Anyone on the street could put an SC/ST Act case against me, unprovoked. Reasons or the validity of the accusations wouldn’t matter. Yet, I would be in jail. This act gives people power to do that. My life would be ruined. I feel targeted, vulnerable and helpless.

Perhaps my personal fears have served as an eye opener to me. They say one can’t understand pain till one personally experiences it. They are correct! Weirdly, now, I see social injustice everywhere. The widespread injustice and oppression. It wrenches my heart.

The strong oppress the weak. Be their strength physical, communal, legal, political, financial. Anything that can be used against another person. The weak are oppressed. Their rights are trampled on. Children, women, poor, the politically weak are all defenseless. They suffer injustice silently.

But not just them. Even others. Successful & rich men too. They are subject to mob justice. The falsely accused. They are ostracized by society or by a media trial. Everyone and anyone is a target!

I’ve painfully discovered that the leaders we are taught to admire are flawed. Their ideas & values are imperfect. Sometimes even rotten; with hate, personal bias or self-interest. Society has adopted many of their ideals without debate. I see clearly that it suffers because of it.

We haven’t been able to build a fair society. Perhaps we never will be able to. The veil is removed.

These realizations infuse doubt & fear. They make me uncomfortable. In some way, they have also paralyzed me. I am unable to act. But I am also unable to ignore them, unable to turn a blind eye.

In the Mahabharata, even the Pandavas faced injustice. They were forced out of their own kingdom with a rigged game of dice. Draupadi was assaulted in her own house. They were all forced to live in forests with meager means. But the story ends with their eventual triumph. That probably restored a sense of justice in us. Maybe even a sense of revenge. But not all subjects are as strong and powerful as the Pandavas. Some may not even have the means to fight.

The Jews. The Korean pleasure women. The citizens of Nanjing. Those defeated by Genghis Khan. Trojan citizens after the war. Indians during foreign rule. The enslaved people across the globe. All were subject to inhumane treatment.

Did all of them eventually triumph over the oppressor? We can’t be sure. I feel maybe not all. We don’t know all their stories. Their stories are lost. All we know is that they must have suffered.

But what really happens to those who suffer? Do their stories end abruptly and ungraciously? What does suffering do to them? Does it make their life meaningless? Is there any meaning in suffering?

What if there is really meaning in suffering? How can we know?

Yet again, whose meaning are we talking about? The victim or witnesses?

So far, I’m not the victim. I am only a witness. Yet I suffer! Mine is the suffering of the witness.

Witnesses suffer too?!

Why do witnesses suffer? Is it out of a fear of being a victim in future? Or is their suffering different? Maybe this is part of what PTSD is: suffering that comes from just the witnessing of violence.

Is there a meaning in suffering? For the victim and the witness?

What is “meaning”? Isn’t meaning another justification that’s personal to the witness? A personal justice. Here we go again. Back to justice.

What’s justice then?

Justice is the idea that society should function according to a pre-accepted set of patterns that we have collectively agreed to. Like, the truth is ascertained beyond doubt. Through evidence or a reliable witness. The perpetrator is restrained. Or punished. The victim is protected, compensated. Or avenged.

This pattern seems comforting. If the story ends this way, it gives us peace. It sits neatly in our minds.

Otherwise, the story disturbs us! It does not sit well with us.

And who are we? We are the witnesses!

Is justice designed for the comfort of the witness or the victim? If there was no witness, the victim may not receive any comfort. Worse still, if the witnesses do not have the power to effect justice, then too the victim does not receive any comfort.

Where then is the comfort of the victim in our justice systems? Is there none?

How does the victim find comfort, then? Possibly in removing themselves from danger, spending time with their loved ones and moving past the trauma. In acceptance & surrender.

Would punishing the perpetrator comfort the victim? It could satisfy the victim’s anger, yes. But would it bring peace and comfort? I doubt that.

What happens to the perpetrator? Do they get to live on forever without consequence? Maybe! Who knows?!
