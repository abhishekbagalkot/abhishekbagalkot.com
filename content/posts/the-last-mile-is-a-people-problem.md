---
title: "The last mile is a people problem"
date: 2026-08-05
summary: "Most last-mile inefficiency isn't in the routing. It's in the twenty minutes before the van leaves and the things drivers know that the system doesn't."
categories: ["Technology"]
---

If you instrument a last-mile operation properly, the distribution of lost time
is rarely where people expect.

The routing is usually fine. Modern engines produce sensible sequences. The
losses cluster in two places: the loading bay before departure, and the
last hundred metres at each stop.

## Before the van leaves

Loading is where plans meet physical reality. The route says twenty-eight drops
in a sequence; the van gets loaded in whatever order the parcels came off the
sorter. Every misordered parcel is a driver climbing over stock at some point
in the day. Nobody measures this because it doesn't appear in any system — it
shows up later, diffused across the day, as "traffic".

## The last hundred metres

The other loss is access. Gate codes, service entrances, which of three
buildings actually takes deliveries, whether reception signs for parcels after
four. Experienced drivers carry this in their heads. It's the single most
valuable dataset in the operation and it's almost never captured, which is why
route quality collapses when a regular driver is off.

Neither problem is a routing problem, and neither is solved by a better solver.
The first is a sequencing handoff between warehouse and transport. The second
is a data capture problem that most operations could fix in a quarter if anyone
owned it.

Optimise the plan all you like. If the van is loaded wrong and the driver
doesn't know the gate code, you've optimised the wrong thing.
