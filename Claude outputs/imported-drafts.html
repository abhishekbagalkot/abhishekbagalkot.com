Career advice for people on top!

2007-12-10 · 673 words · Business · career-advice-for-people-on-top.md · original

Well getting on top is tough. But what happens when you get there? There are instances in life where you can get to the top very fast. So you are working for a start up. Within a couple of years you proved yourself to be one of the top performers. What next? So you started a company and worked hard and got somewhere. What you dreamed years ago has finally come true. What next? What happens when you reach the top slot early? Most people have a set of core competencies that are defined in the early years of their career. It is very unlikely that they will change very soon. For example if you are a marketer, and a serious one at that, there is very little chance that you will turn to programming after becoming the head of the marketing department. You will continue to sell. If you are a senior project manager for a construction firm, there is very little chance that you will jump to insurance. You will probably not get into client relations or other functions that are not related to construction at all. Before you make that leap from one job description to another, one needs to stop and think. In every vocation there comes a time when one has done enough of job X and has explored it enough. Now he wants something new. It happens naturally. The problem arises when you are already on top doing what you are doing. Then you don’t know what to do next. Then one just goes on increasing the volume of one’s work instead of choosing higher directions. Form handling 2 projects to 10 projects. Form handling two business units to 15 business units. Though this is career growth, in the long run it is stagnating, uninteresting and less fun. I suspect this happens with most young entrepreneurs, leads in small companies and senior management where the next career path is not very well defined. If you are facing something similar, read on.

In most achievers there is a need for a challenge. Their creative juices flow out of the impossibility lying ahead. Take the challenge out and you will kill the performer. Then they will become below average performers. Achievers need to be challenged. Continuously. But if you are on top, there is hardly anyone to challenge you. You need to challenge yourself. So here is the recipe: Replace yourself at what you are doing and do something that nobody around has ever done before. This advice comes in two pieces. One replace yourself. Two do something new. And it is imperative you do them together. Doing one without two is not safe and two with one is Herculean.

Replace Yourself: If you don’t want to stagnate, you need to do new things. But you cannot just stop doing what you were doing before. Somebody, somewhere has to still do those tasks. So get somebody junior to do your job. Throw open your tasks to a couple of guys and start teaching them. Teaching is an inevitable part of leading. To lead well and to lead from the front, you need to do things nobody else could and then teach them to others. This way your whole unit is constantly learning new things. It is on a continuous learning cure and having fun learning them. Replace yourself slowly, gradually. Gently pass your responsibilities to the juniors.

Do something new: The reason you are on top, is because you are good. But good people can’t escape from getting better. You will have to learn new things. Select your next big target and go ahead. Select something that is realistic but challenging. And go full blast.

Caution! It is important to be very good at your job before you change your job role. Make sure you have done what you are good at long enough before every change. Career skills take time to master.

All the best! Go ahead and create your self the most unique job in the word.

The team and the customer

2007-06-27 · 308 words · Business · the-team-and-the-customer.md · original

Right now I am working on my first real assignment. We code named it RAStik. I am supposed to develop a software product. We are a team of four people. Three developers and one tester. Plus we had a specialist R&D guy to do our R&D for us before we started. In the beginning I was the requirements guy. I spoke to the customer and the project financiers and wrote a Requirements Document for the product. Everybody agreed. We had some clarity now on what we needed to learn. So we set up the R&D guy to start his part of the job. Then I prepared the time estimates, and the financiers got involved in a price discussion. There were a few disagreeables. We made a few changes. We stripped the application of a few features and stopped the R&D guy from doing his R&D in those areas. By the time the R&D guy finished, we had the project financiers agree upon the time and effort estimations as well. So we set up the new team with the new time and effort estimations…….. the team is now working on this. In the beginning, I played a very active role in the development. Is till do. But I have now noticed something. I noticed that it was not sufficient if I was the only one in charge. I feel it is too much burden on one person. I want the boys to be directly responsible for a few things apart from development. I want the boys to be directly responsible for customer satisfaction. I want them to see what effect their code is producing in whose life? I want them to take charge of improving the customer’s life. In most development cycles the team does not directly feel responsible for customer satisfaction. That is a bad practice. I think.

Fixing the bike

A few few days ago a fresh guilt took hold of me….. Of not getting my motorbike serviced. I had missed the second service by months. My heart was bleeding. On 07-07-07, “no more” I said. I took my bike to service. I took an entire day off and decided to spend the whole day at the garage. “I’ll learn something” I told myself. Must be a guilty biker’s consciousness on the prowl.

I reached the garage at 11 in the morning. The mechanic said that he will start only by 2:30 or so. I went back home and came back at 2:30.

I look at the mechanic and say “Aap se seekhne ke liye aaya mein” (I have come here to learn from you) “Zaroor sahaab” He says. (Of course) I tell him that I keep traveling a to distant places frequently. I need to learn about the bike in case something happens on the way. He just nods.

The mechanic is an under middle aged man called Sahrief. He is short and has light facial hair that he doesn’t shave. He also wears a plastic rimmed pair of glasses that he uses to look carefully at the smaller and finer components. He is confident and answers to the point. He respects his customers. His customers respect him. He owns the shop and asks his 18 year old son for help. He soon plans to send his son for a professional degree. His shop consists of one room with all kinds of spare parts lying around. There is only enough room for one to move around to fetch the spares. There is no arrangement to sit anywhere. Chairs are only laid outside the shop. The outside of his shop is a yard shaded by trees. The shop is just a score feet away from the noisy Tar Bund Traffic Square. But under the tree, in front of the shop, there is a sleepy afternoon feeling of quite towns.

I am sitting there waiting for the service to begin. Sharief is doing all kinds of things. He has opened my fork and sent it for getting straightened up. He is brought a new shade for my headlight and is preparing to mount it on the headlights. He is greasing my shock absorbers. But the real service….. the absolute real thing where the bike is opened, the engine oil changed, the gearbox oil levels checked and refilled, the transmission re-lubricated, the chains tightened and all that jazz that makes the real service…….. that doesn’t start. Meanwhile I am fiddling my my friends camera, trying to take a few snaps of the whole garage. I am trying to keep my self occupied. A lot of people come and go to the shop. Somebody asks for waste oil that he sells for a hundred bucks a can. Some are his friends who have come to say hello.

At around 4 in the afternoon, a middle aged man with sparse white hair, rides into the garage on this black bullet. He is smartly dressed. Too smart for a visit to the garage. He wears a tummy that he covers with his belt. And like most men who were 21 in the seventies, he wears his trousers well above the navel. He picks up a chair without saying anything, sits on it, removes a cigarette pack from the pockets, lights it and starts smoking. He hasn’t said anything so far. Sharief looks at him but doesn’t say anything. He is niether approving or disapproving of him. I dislike him instantly.

“Ahh! It’s cold here” he says. Sharief looks at him and smiles inside. “Won’t find the Saudi climate here” he says, teasingly. They were old friends I guessed. Mush have been friends for decades. Without saying anything the new man, sports out his mobile, opens an old fashioned address book from his pocket and dials a number. “Nazim speaking” he says loudly and enquirers about somebody. It is a wrong number. He gives up. Sharief is almost laughing now. He looks at his son to share his amusement. He smiles back.

“Today is a very lucky day” Nazim says all of a sudden. “The date is 7-7-7. I heard it all over the news. They say, whatever you buy today will bring you luck. So I went immediately and bought a couple of shirts. They were expensive. But I did not care. Today is a lucky day after all.” Sharief doesn’t say anything. “My wife plans to buy some furniture today. She doesn’t want to wait for the less expensive ones to arrive. Today is too lucky. Will go out in the evening with her to buy them.” “Are they made up of teak?” Sharief asks, smiling. Nazim says something about what they are made up of and goes into another round of explaining how expensive they are.

Suddenly Sahrief looks at my bike odometer and says, “Arrey, the bike reading is 2777.0 kms. 777 again. What a coincidence!” He looks at Nazim and smiles. “Fantastic!” shouts Nazim, lighting another cigarette. “Must be a really lucky bike.” Suddenly his attention shifts to me. “Don’t ever sell this bike to anybody” he says addressing me. “It is really lucky. 2777 kms on 07-07-07. Really…….. very lucky” I smile back, not knowing what to do. I am a little shy to say anything. I don’t want to participate. Not today.

A few conversations begin between Sahrief and Nazim that I don’t understand. It is mostly Nazim who does the talking. Sahrief is bending over the bike and is constantly occupied. He is answering in monosyllables. But the smile doesn’t leave his face. Nazim is explaining a lot of things to Sahiref. About his experiences with the customs when he had gone to Saudi, about how many things he bought for everybody from there. Sahrief is listening to him, still smiling. By now their friendship has caught my fancy. I wonder how Sahrief, a man of few words, is persistently listening to this boastful friendly man. And I see that they have probably been friends for a long time. But what a contrast of men! I began to think how Sahreif could have even tolerated such a noisy person? On the contrary, he was enjoying it! He would listen to a long discourse about something completely unrelated to him. Probably most of them were made up. He even enjoyed it. But he never stopped working. Then I understood.

Sahrief is a mechanic. And a good one at that. His bread and butter and respect comes from working on motorbikes. And he has to be good. To be good he has to pay attention. He has to pay attention to the bikes. He has to LISTEN to them, LISTEN to what they need. Besides he loves them. He understands them. He has spent his entire life with them. When he is working, he can’t be doing anything else. His commitment is too intense. With this man, he can he does not need to participate to have a conversation. He just needs to listen. Nazim does not ask him to express his views, he only wants hearing. Sharief only listens, Nazim only talks. And in this non uniform participation, their friendship works for both. It is because they are so different, that they got along so well.

By now Sharief is done with all the minor jobs and sends his son to take the bike for a wash. After the wash, he will begin the real work on the bike. He sits down with Nazim for the first time. Nazim is still talking about something.

Finally Sahrief says “I am planning to buy a Gold ring. Should I get it from Saudi? They sat it is cheap and there. I’ll just get a lump of gold from there and make it into a ring here.” “Don’t.” says Nazim. Shareif looks at him questioningly. “They will steal from you when you get it done here” he says. ‘They’ being the goldsmiths. “I know a goldsmith personally!” “Even your brother will steal from you” says Nazim. “I can give you my word. Your own brother will steal from you if it is Saudi gold.” “Then? What do I do?” asks Sharief confused. “Get a lump of gold and sell it here. Then by yourself a ring from the money” says Nazim.

Sharief is impressed with Nazim now. He has told him a problem about goldsmiths he never knew. And he has also produced the solution right away. He is impressed. For Sharief, Nazim is a man of the world. He has traveled to Saudi. He is a smooth talker. He narrates his stories and opinions openly to Sahrief. He is smart in the ways of the world. Nazim understands a game completely unknown to Sharief. Often he tells stories of unknown people in unknown lands which Sahrief listens with bewilderment. Open mouthed. Even in his middle age, Sahrief has not lost sense of awe.

The rules of the game that Nazim talks of are foreign to him. Sharief is a man of physics. Of Physics, he knows and understands the rules. He may not know how to express what he knows in mathematical equations, but he knows what will fix the bike and what will break it. But the world outside the bikes, the world of men, of transactions of wealth, of countries beyond, he is ignorant. Nazim brings him stories of this world.

“Oh! I remember something” says Nazim with a start. “I am having a problem with my bike. Why don’t you take a look?” He takes Sahrief to his bike and they both examine the problem. I watch them from a distance. They both bend over the bike. When they come back to the shop, Nazim is looks worried. He has just remembered a problem he had forgotten. “What to do?” He asks. “Bring the bike one day when you are free. I’ll fix that problem in an hour” says Sharief. He sounds confident. He KNOWS the problem. He knows how to fix it and how long it will take. He is back in the world he belongs to.

The “real” service began only by 5. At 5, Sharief sits down with my bike and starts working on it. For the next 2 hours he did not speak except for giving instructions to his son.

15 year olds

2007-06-19 · 816 words · — uncategorised — · 15-year-olds.md · original

When I graduated from standard 7 to standard 8 we had a new teacher to teach us Physics. I still remember her name was Kalpana. We used to call her “Kalpana Miss”. She taught very well. She answered every question honestly. She made sure you understood it. She encouraged asking questions. What was more she loved me. I was the most mischievous of the whole class. But I was never punished. I was also the guy who asked the most questions. And also the guy who paid he most attention to every concept. But only till I understood it.

We had a great time in her class. She had a knack of covering a lot of concepts rapidly. But her classes never seemed cluttered or hurried. Probably because she herself understood the subject with clarity. And whatever she could not answer, she asked us to discuss with her after the class.

I still remember one topic very well. We were studying REFRACTION. Why mirages occur in deserts? It was optics again. She explained that land on the deserts gets heated up very rapidly and starts heating up the air above. Since air is a bad conductor of heat, it is a little sloppy in transferring the same heat to the air lying above it. Hence the lower layers of air, the ones closer to the land, were always hotter than the ones above. Due to irregular expansion under these differential temperature conditions, different layers of air would develop different mass densities. And hence different optical densities as well.

When blue light entered these layers from from the sky, it would skew a little and bend away from its normal course. Every time it entered a new density layer, it would skew a little more. After a series of continual bending, it would finally start moving upwards away from the ground.

That was cool! Light bends so much due to refraction, that it starts moving upwards after it has suffered enough refraction. It never hits the sand at all. So it can’t have the color of sand. It still has the color of the sky. Blue. And when such light enters our eyes, we see it as blue light coming from the land. It looks like water. That is a mirage.

I was listening intently. And as soon as she finished, I jumped with a question.

“Light only skews away from the normal when it is enters a layer of different optical density. But onces, the light is skewed enough that it is almost horizontal to the ground, it has no differential optical densities to cross. So it should just move horizontal. Why does it move upwards?” I said. Now that was a big sounding question. Immediately the class lost interest. But I was persistent. She tried to convince me that that is how it is. But I never gave in. I went on arguing.

“Light bends so much due to refraction, that it starts moving upwards after sometime” she said again. “But light has no reason to skew anymore once it is completely horizontal. It doesn’t cross any optical differentials anymore. So it should NOT bend upwards.”

This was clearly a dent in her argument. So a few boys took notice that maybe I was serious about this. And maybe I was even right. So another guy stood up. His name was Siddharth. He tried to explain me what the teacher was saying. But I insisted that it doesn’t break my argument. After sometime another guy stood up. His name was Anindo. He was a very cool headed guy. He also said something about the problem at hand. And we all got into an argument. Siddharth and Anindo on one side, and me on another. We were defending our own point of views like professors who were involved in a big debate. It felt like we were working on something very important. We wracked our brains very hard. We gave it everything we had. We were 15 years old.

Anindo finally said, “I don’t think we can say anything against Abhishek’s argument.”

And the argument ended abruptly. We all understood that we don’t understand this completely. We all agreed that the argument is given in the book was flawed.

I wonder now, how it must have felt then. I had put the whole class against me. I did not care what they thought. I argued with my teachers if I felt they were wrong. I never gave in until I understood and agreed with them. When I was 15, I had a lot of courage and confidence. I trusted my head.

Years later when I was discussing the same with my friend, he pointed out that the reversal of direction of light was because of what we called Total Internal Reflection and was not just a case of ordinary refraction. Then it all fit into place.

My father used to keep getting transfered from one city to another due to the nature of his job, and I had to jump to and fro across a lot of schools. I remember very clearly. Stop fooling around” at any one who would try to make mischief. She probably did not notice how much more mischief her “Stop fooling around” caused. She picked up the physics book and asked somebody from the class to read a paragraph from it. Then she asked a question from the same paragraph. She waited for a while looking if anyone could answer that. If nobody was forth coming, she would answer it herself and she would ask somebody else to read the next paragraph. She looked like a very strict and stern lady to me. But I did no understand why the other kids did not think so. They kept giggling and throwing things at each other. “Stop fooling around” she would yell again and get along with her job. Evey now and then somebody would run across from his seat to some other seat. Then she would say “Stop fooling around, Rahul” or “Stop fooling around, Tanushree”.

Anyways, we were reading a chapter from optics. Somewhere the book said, when light enter your eyes, it forms an inverted image of the object on your retina. So she asked, “when light enter your eyes it form an inverted image on your retina. Then how do we see everything straight?” The answer was not in the book. The class went silent. The eyes see everything inverted! Nobody could stomach that.

I knew the answer. I had seen it on TV. I rose my hand. “Yes” she said looking at me. I stood up. “The brain inverts it for us again” I said. “Yes. The brain inverts it for us. Stop fooling around Dhiraj. Next paragraph Atul. Stop fooling around”

I sat down. I was very very proud. I knew something the whole class did not know. And it was only my first day. It was a simple thing. But I felt very proud. Then I decided, Physics was MY cup of tea. I was always good at it. The same year I learned about magnets and magnetic field of the earth. That same year I read the Newtons laws of Physics. I went crazy. I was in class when somebody read the paragraph. It was like, something flew out of the book and hit me. I was dumbfounded. I read them and I understood them and I was spellbound. I realized I could now explain a lot of things. I could explain why a bigger ball has to be pushed harder to move. Why I can throw my younger brother so easily but he can’t throw me. Why I felt like I was moving backwards on the seat when my dad accelerated the scooter. Everything was falling into place. The same year I learned of moment of force. And I discovered why I could close my front gate so easily when I pushed furthest from the hinge. It was a very exiting time!

The next year, somebody else was supposed to teach us Physics. I still remember her name was Kalpana. We used to call her “Kalpana Miss”. She taught very well. She answered every question honestly. She made sure you understood it. She encouraged asking questions. What was more she loved me. I was the most mischievous of the whole class. But I was never punished. I was also the guy who asked the most questions. And also the guy who paid he most attention to every concept. But only till I understood it.

The Swim

2007-05-07 · 719 words · — uncategorised — · the-swim.md · original

I love swimming. I remember waking up early in those chilly winter mornings reaching the pool after a shivering ride, taking a shower and that chilly first dip. The first five minutes were the hardest when my body had to produce heat to make up for the what was being so rapidly lost to the water in the pool. But then it got better and then I cold swim and enjoy myself. I set myself daily targets of 10 laps which I gradually increased to 20. I could never manage to lift my head out of water while I swam. So I had to complete a lap before I lost my breath, catch a breath and then go on with the next lap. I would get scared of what would happen if I lost my breath in the middle of the lap in the deep. But still I went ahead and did it. Every day I came back feeling exhausted and refreshed at the same time. Perhaps the most relaxing part of swimming is that my body was horizontal; piercing through the water. So much different from how I spent most of my time on land, standing straight or sitting on chair where the body is straight and erect. For a change the feeling of moving with my body horizontal is exiting. It gave me a peek at how much my body can do. I swam as much as I could and then stopped at the corner bar panting and catching my breath. I enjoyed it when I pushed my body to the limits. And when the limit was reached I would sit back and look at how my body was feeling. Examining it clinically as if it were not a part of me, not caring how well I did I would ponder about how well or badly my body had taken it. Whether it was my calf or my shoulders were hurting badly and which part of my body had performed better than the other. I was happy then examining the details of the event inside my head, without really caring if I did well or not. I felt relaxed, challenged, exited and calm at the same time. There was never a target, but still there was a point in that whole exercise.

I wonder if I can look at my entire life with the same perspective. Situations come and go. Sometimes I react and sometimes I pro-act. And in all this that happens, I wonder why I can’t look at the whole even with just as much detachment as I did when I swam. Perhaps I care more about these events in life than I do about how many laps I can take. But why do I do that? While I swam, if I failed to reach the other end in time before I lost my breath I could drown and even die. I know how remote a possibility that is. But sometime when the instructor is not around these things happen. What is it that made me risk my life happily but makes me miserable when I risk something else. Say my job or my honor among my acquaintances. Why does it make me so miserable when my boss thinks I did a lousy job at something? Why does it take so much of me out? So does it mean I care of these things more than I care for my body? Why can’t I just look at these events as swimming lessons for the mind, risking this and that every now and then? Sitting back after every lap examining how I did, I would say to myself “My ego seems to have taken a beating today” or “Shux! My self esteem is hurting” It may sound funny, but it is not out of the world. But we just don’t live like that. We seem to be far more participating in anything the mind is involved with. Ranting and raging at every false step we make. Trying so hard to look good at everything we do. Perhaps it is because we look at the mind as more of a part of ourselves than the body. And whatever happens to the mind happens to us. We tell ourselves “I am not the body, I am the mind”.

Rainy Day

2007-09-25 · 682 words · — uncategorised — · rainy-day.md · original

It is eight o clock in the evening. I am walking out of my house. I climb two stories down and step in between the gates of my building. I open my umbrella. It is raining. A pond of water has collected just in front of my house. I step into it. It is a good feeling. The water is cool. Rain drops fall on my umbrella. I like the sound. As the the drops fall on the ground, they shine under the road lights momentarily. Then they disappear. Some drops fall on the ponds formed here and there. They disperse fresh small waves. I am enjoying the rain. I have dropped my disgust of getting wet. It seems less relevant today. And as I walk, enjoy the sound of rain everywhere. On the trees, on the grass and sometimes on my feet.

I am thinking about a girl. I like thinking of her. I like the idea of falling in love with her. She is very lovely. Very lovable. I remember her thin face. I think of her and my heartbreaks come chasing. But somehow, in her thoughts the heart ache seems less painful. She is making me fall in love with her toady. In some small way, I am falling in love all over again. I am smiling.

I reach the pan shop. “Maza aagaya baarish mein, Salim bhai.” I say. “Haan sir. Bahut bhaari baarish ho rahi hai” says he.

He opens a pack of cigarettes and forwards one to me. I light it and ask for a coffee. He makes it and gives it to me. I sit on rather tall stool. The shop is on one corner a cross road. There a graveyard on the opposite side. To the right side of the graveyard is a school and in front of that a few small shops of second hand metal. But they are all closed now. So the cross road is quite deserted. I sit facing the crossroad, the shop on my right side. My right hand rests on the shop counter as I place my cup over it in between my sips. I smoke with my left hand. I relax into the stool. I enjoy being alone in the place very alone. Secluded.

And then in a moment, I feel quiet. I feel alive. There is clarity in this feeling. At that moment I don’t regret anything. I am conscious of my feelings. But it does not disturb me. I sip my coffee, I smoke my cigarette. I am alone, quiet and happy.

“Iftaar hogaya?” I ask Salim bhai. “Haan sir” he says. And silence follows. Silence.

“Kuch awaazein aa rahi hain” Salim bahi says, as if only to break the silence. “Hmm…..” I dont pay much attention. But I am curious. I start listening. What sounds? I wonder. “Aurat ki aawaazein aa rahi hain” he says again. “Kahan se?”

He doesn’t reply. And then I hear it. The first time I hear it, I know it is a woman. That is all I now. Second time I hear it, it is completely different. It is full of pain, that voice. It is terrorized. It is a scream of a woman in intense pain and agony. The helplessness in the voice has given it a certain quality. A quality that inspires fear.

“Haan. Aaa rahi hai . Kahan se?” I ask. “School se aa rahi hongi. Zhagada hota raheta hai wahan” he says. “Kaun reheta hai waan?” “School ke owner rehete hai” And there is another scream. Louder and more helpless than the others before. “Maar raha hoga” he says.

I want to mention to him, what kind of education could a merciless fool like that give to the children. But I keep quiet. I walk away. When I walk back, I can still hear the screams of that woman. Inside my head. I hear them over and over again. The hatred in the world has come face to face to me. Hatred not for armed men. But hatred of a man for his own wife.
